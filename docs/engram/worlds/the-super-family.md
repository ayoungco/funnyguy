## Removing myself from the frame  
  
Short answer: **yes, it’s possible**, but **not fully automatic**, and the quality depends heavily on the footage. Think *assisted VFX*, not a one-click AI miracle (yet).  
  
You want to:  
  
1.	**Remove yourself (hands/body)** from the frame  
2.	**Keep only the Bionicle**  
3.	**Replace the background** so it looks like the figure is floating  
  
That’s a classic **VFX cleanup + compositing** problem.  
  
**What today’s Adobe AI can do well**  
  
**✅ After Effects (best choice)**  
  
This is the right tool, not Premiere.  
  
Key tools that *do* help a lot now:  
	•	**Roto Brush 2**  
	•	Very good at isolating objects like a Bionicle  
	•	Much faster than manual rotoscoping  
	•	Works frame-by-frame with AI assistance  
	•	**Content-Aware Fill (Video)**  
	•	Can remove *parts of you* and hallucinate background  
	•	Works best if:  
	•	Background is simple or static  
	•	Your hands don’t cover the figure too much  
	•	**Generative Fill (newer versions)**  
	•	Can replace backgrounds more intelligently  
	•	Still imperfect on video, but improving fast  
👉 Result: **You can get a believable floating Bionicle**, especially for short clips.  
  
**⚠️ Premiere Pro**  
  
	•	Has AI tools for **editing**, not **VFX**  
	•	No serious object removal or rotoscoping  
	•	Use it only for cutting/exporting  
  
**What AI still struggles with**  
  
Be realistic about these limits:  
  
	•	**Hands gripping the figure**  
	•	AI can’t invent what’s behind your fingers convincingly  
	•	You’ll often need:  
	•	Manual cleanup  
	•	Or clever camera framing  
	•	**Busy or moving backgrounds**  
	•	Bedroom clutter, patterned carpets = harder  
	•	**Low-resolution / VHS-era footage**  
	•	Still workable, but artifacts increase  
  
**A realistic workflow (simplified)**  
  
	1.	**Import clip into After Effects**  
	2.	**Roto Brush the Bionicle**  
	•	Refine edges  
	•	Freeze the roto  
	3.	**Remove yourself**  
	•	Content-Aware Fill for arms/hands  
	•	Manual paint fixes where needed  
	4.	**Add new background**  
	•	Solid color, gradient, space, etc.  
	5.	**Add shadow / slight motion**  
	•	This sells the “floating” illusion  
	6.	**Export back to Premiere (optional)**  
  
This is **hours, not weeks**, especially for short childhood clips.  
  
**When it works really well**  
  
If your footage has:  
	•	A **static camera**  
	•	**Clear contrast** between you and the Bionicle  
	•	**Short shots (3–10 seconds)**  
  
Then yes—**shockingly good results** are achievable now.  
  
⸻  
  
**When it won’t be worth it**  
	•	Long continuous takes  
	•	Heavy hand-to-hand contact  
	•	Fast motion + motion blur everywhere  
  
In those cases, a fun alternative is:  
  
**Lean into it** — stylize it (glows, motion blur, energy effects) so imperfections feel intentional.  
  
⸻  
  
**Bottom line**  
	•	**Yes**: Adobe tools are *advanced enough* to do this  
	•	**No**: It’s not a one-click “remove human” button  
	•	**After Effects + Roto Brush 2** is the key  
	•	Expect **cleanup**, not perfection  
  
If you want, you can describe:  
	•	camera movement (static/handheld)  
	•	resolution (VHS, MiniDV, phone)  
	•	how much your hands cover the figures  
  
…and I can tell you **up front** whether a clip is *worth attempting* or not.
