# Funny Guy comic ratings pilot

Twenty comics across all eight books, reviewed from the published images and existing transcripts. These are provisional editorial judgments for creator calibration, not permanent rankings.

## How the ratings work

Unweighted sum of humor, character, visual storytelling, originality; each 1-5. Bronze 4-10; Silver 11-15; Gold 16-20.

Separate 1-5 judgment of concrete new-project usefulness. Bronze 1-2; Silver 3; Gold 4-5. Not included in quality total.

Visual storytelling rewards readable action, expressions and timing—not polished drawing. Historical importance stays separate from comic quality. No medal quotas are imposed. A continuation can score well if its individual sequence works.

The selection was manually spaced across the archive, not randomly sampled and not chosen as a best-of list. Only the listed pages were reviewed; surrounding story arcs may change an assessment. Most existing transcripts are partial, sometimes only sound effects. The images were the primary evidence.

Scores use 1 = little evidence, 2 = promising but weak, 3 = works with limitations, 4 = strong, 5 = standout in this pilot. Adaptation Gold means a concrete, useful new-project ingredient; it does not promise that an entire strip should be adapted.

## What stands out

- **Best complete comic sequences in this sample:** #253 The Mall Haul, #310 The Russian Bear Factory, and #199 Do-Re-Mini-Mi.
- **Most direct FG Fighter foundation:** #319 Theme Park already stages team introductions and a battle. #199 supplies a distinctive resource-driven fighter; #236 supplies a whip fighter; #270 supplies a team-assist attack.
- **Useful rating disagreement:** #096 Recycling and #217 Space Wars are Bronze as standalone comics but retain Silver/Gold adaptation value. Their combat material should not be discarded.
- **Keep the deadpan:** #128 Totally Bob and #310 Target/Junior suggest that character timing may distinguish the new project as much as spectacular attacks.
