
Yes — rescanning is probably worth it if:

* the originals still exist
* the line art has sentimental or archival value
* the old scans were done with early consumer hardware/software
* you want to future-proof them for animation/video/printing/AI workflows

But do it strategically.

Your old 300 DPI scans are likely already “good enough” for:

* web
* video
* panel extraction
* archival reference

300 DPI at US Letter is already substantial.

The question is less “resolution” and more:

* tonal range
* contrast
* paper texture
* alignment
* compression artifacts
* scanner dynamic range
* whether you scanned grayscale vs color

⸻

Modern rescans can look dramatically better because:

Better scanners/software

Modern flatbeds:

* reduce noise
* preserve pencil better
* recover faded ink
* reduce shadowing near binding

Better workflow

You now know:

* how to level artwork
* crop consistently
* preserve margins
* avoid over-thresholding

Better post-processing

Today you can:

* batch denoise
* preserve paper grain intentionally
* separate line art from paper
* vectorize selectively
* upscale intelligently

⸻

My recommendation

DO NOT overwrite the originals

Treat old scans as:

archive/master-v1/

New scans:

archive/master-v2/

You may later prefer:

* old paper tone
* old scanner artifacts
* raw childhood aesthetic

Never destroy provenance.

⸻

Best archival workflow now

Scan:

* 600 DPI
* grayscale for pencil/ink
* color if markers/highlighters exist
* TIFF or PNG
* NO JPEG

600 DPI is the sweet spot.

1200 DPI usually becomes:

* enormous
* slower
* diminishing returns

unless:

* tiny detailed ink
* halftones
* print reproduction
* preservation-grade archival work

⸻

Important scanner setting:

TURN OFF:

* auto sharpening
* dust removal
* auto contrast
* OCR enhancement
* descreen

Consumer scanner software destroys artwork with “helpful” processing.

You want:

* raw
* flat
* neutral

Then edit later.

⸻

Very important:

Keep the paper texture.

Do NOT aggressively threshold everything into pure black/white.

The aged paper + graphite texture may become aesthetically powerful now.

Especially for:

* slideshow films
* analog vibe
* liminal archival feeling
* documentary-style presentation

A little paper grain and aging can actually elevate the work.

⸻

Suggested modern pipeline

Archive master

600dpi TIFF

Working version

Generate:

PNG cleaned

Derived assets

Then:

* auto panel extraction
* video generation
* web gallery
* EPUB/CBZ
* motion comic

One more thing

You mentioned:

“they’ve aged”

That may actually be an asset.

Old paper:

* yellowing
* faded graphite
* uneven tones
* margin marks
* accidental wear

can create an authenticity modern digital art often lacks.

You may discover the physical object is now artistically more interesting than the original intent.

Especially in motion-video format with:

* slow zooms
* projector hum
* ambient audio
* narration

It can become archival cinema rather than “just comics.”
