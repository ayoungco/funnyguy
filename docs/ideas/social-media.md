Yes, you should absolutely do it. The modern internet thrives on niche, deeply realized worlds, and treating your project like an archive of an alternate universe or a living artifact is an incredibly compelling way to present it.
You don't need a massive archive of new content to begin; you just need to frame what you have with intention. Here is how to look at the narrative strategy and how to best use those AI workflows without losing the soul of your original pencil work.
1. The Strategy: The "Living Archive"
Since it has been years since you actively worked on the project, don't try to position the account as a traditional webcomic that updates every Tuesday. Instead, treat it like a found-footage project or a digital codex.
The Meta-Narrative: Frame the account as an in-universe catalog, a collection of "recovered documents," or a creator’s retrospective.
The Maturity Effect: The fact that the work is years old actually works in your favor. It gives the lore a layer of dust and history. You can lean into a slightly surreal, archival tone that matches how people consume deep lore on the modern web (think analog horror or worldbuilding wiki-dives).
2. Navigating the AI Workflow
The available tools for taking scanned pencil sketches and elevating them are incredible right now, but there is a major trap: over-processing can strip away your hand-drawn style.
Because these were originally pencil comics, the texture of your graphite lines holds a lot of character. Here is a balanced workflow to keep the soul intact while making the art presentation modern:
The "Clean and Enhance" Pipeline
Instead of asking an AI to completely redraw your panels from scratch (which often sterilizes the character designs), use a hybrid approach:
The Base Prep (Upscaling & Leveling): Use standard tools or AI upscalers (like Krea or Topaz) to enhance the resolution of your scans. Run a crisp high-contrast level adjustment so your whites are clean and your pencil lines pop.
AI Object Removal for Cleanup: Tools like VeryUtils AI or standard Photoshop Generative Fill are perfect for wiping away scanner smudges, dust, or old coffee stains on the paper without touching your actual line art.  
VeryUtils
ControlNet / Structure Reference: If you do want to convert a pencil sketch into a fully colored or inked digital illustration, use an AI tool that supports Structure Control (like Adobe Firefly’s Structure Reference or Stable Diffusion's ControlNet). This locks down your exact pencil outlines while letting the AI handle things like flat colors, lighting, or background shading.
3. How to Structure the Page
Instagram is highly visual, but worldbuilding requires context. Split the weight evenly between the image and the text:
The Grid: Post a mix of single panels, multi-slide carousel "chapters," and isolated character/artifact concept sketches. Let the grid look like a messy, beautiful workspace.
The Captions: Use the text space to build the world. Write your captions partially in-universe (explaining the lore behind the drawing) and partially out-of-universe (reflecting on your mindset when you drew it years ago).
Starting the page gives that internal universe a sandbox to live in, and seeing the old pages cleaned up in a public space might just spark the momentum to create something entirely new for it.
